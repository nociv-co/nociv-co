1What should it do?

Tap + for each thing you want. We pick the parts. The brain is a Raspberry Pi Pico.

2How big?

Pick how many 20 mm squares the lid has. Parts snap to half squares.

3Place your parts

This is the lid, seen from the top. Each part takes the squares it really needs. Drag it to move. Yellow means it does not fit there.

4Look at it

Spin it with one finger. Pinch to zoom.

5Get your files

  1. Print both files flat, no supports. PLA works. The box prints open side up.
  2. Push the parts through the lid and tighten their nuts. Glue the speaker under its holes. Press the printed knobs onto the knob shafts.
  3. Screw the Pico onto its 4 posts, USB toward the side hole.
  4. Wire it up, then screw the lid on.

What you need

    Next: code it. Writing the code works on your phone. Loading it onto the Pico needs a computer with Chrome or Edge and a USB cable. Coming soon.

    No 3D printer? Send the files to a print service, or print at a library or makerspace.
